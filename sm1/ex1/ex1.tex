\section{Analytical problems}
\subsection{Ising model with nearest + next nearest interaction}
The hamiltonian for the Ising model with nearest + next nearest interaction is
\begin{equation}
    H = -J_1 \sum_i \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} - J_2 \sum_i \sigma_i \sigma_{i+2} - B \sum_i \sigma_i
\end{equation}
Since the boundries are negligable in the thermodynamic limit, we can select periodic boundary conditions $N + 1 = 1, N + 2 = 2$. The partition function is then
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    Z_N = \sum_{\sigma_1 = \pm 1} \cdot\cdot\cdot \sum_{\sigma_N = \pm 1}
    \exp\left[\beta J_1 \sum_i \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} + \beta J_2 \sum_i \sigma_i \sigma_{i+2} + \beta B \sum_i \sigma_i\right] =
    \\
    = \sum_{\sigma_1 = \pm 1} \cdot\cdot\cdot \sum_{\sigma_N = \pm 1}
    \prod_i \exp\left(
        \beta J_1 \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} +
        \beta J_2 \sigma_i \sigma_{i+2} +
        \beta B \sigma_i
    \right)
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
To create a transfer matrix, let us define a superspin $\ket{\bar{\sigma}_i} \in \R^4$ out of every two consecutive spins:
\begin{equation}
    \ket{\uparrow \uparrow} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \qquad
    \ket{\uparrow \downarrow} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \qquad
    \ket{\downarrow \uparrow} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \qquad
    \ket{\downarrow \downarrow} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
Which means the exponent in equation 2 is
\begin{equation}
    \exp\left(
        \beta J_1 \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} +
        \beta J_2 \sigma_i \sigma_{i+2} +
        \beta B \sigma_i
    \right) = \bra{\bar{\sigma}_i}M\ket{\bar{\sigma}_{i+1}}
\end{equation}
Where $M$ is the transfer matrix:
\begin{equation}
    M = \exp \left[\beta \begin{pmatrix}
        J_1 + J_2 + B & J_1 - J_2 + B & & \\
        -J_1 + J_2 + B & -J_1 - J_2 + B & & \\
        & & -J_1 - J_2 - B & -J_1 + J_2 - B \\
        & & J_1 - J_2 - B & J_1 + J_2 - B
    \end{pmatrix}\right]
\end{equation}
Naming the four vectors from equation 3 $b_1, ... b_4$, the partition function is
\begin{equation}
    Z_N = \sum_{\hat{\sigma}_1 = b_1 ... b_4} \cdot\cdot\cdot \sum_{\hat{\sigma}_N = b_1 ... b_4}
    \bra{\bar{\sigma}_1}M\ket{\bar{\sigma}_2}
    \bra{\bar{\sigma}_2}M\ket{\bar{\sigma}_3}
    \dots
    \bra{\bar{\sigma}_{N/4}}M\ket{\bar{\sigma}_1} = Tr(M^{N/4})
\end{equation}
Where in the last equation we used the fact that $M$ is translationaly invariant. Since the trance is base independent, we can epxress it as the sum of the matrix's eigenvalues, and in the thermodynamic limit, all eigenvalues except the largest one will be negliable, and the partition function is
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{N\rightarrow \infty} Z_N = \lambda_{max}^N
\end{equation}
This result makes sense because it gives us extensive free energy.
\subsection{Ising model as a Lattice Gas}
Given seven sites and three particles that could be in those sites, there is a $\binom{7}{3}$ options for their configuration, but we can make the situation much simpler. The first thing to notice is the translation invariance, so we can assume the first particle is in the first position, and simply multiply the options by 7 at the end. This should leave us with $\binom{7}{3} / 7 = 5$ options, which is a very managable number. Now let's look at the positions of the other two particles. We don't want to overcount by saying that placing the particles in positions $(1, 6, 7)$ is distinct from placing them in $(1,2,3)$. Those two situations are the same under translations, which we already acounted for. We can find duplicates by enumartion and remove them. The positions that are left after removing all duplicates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    b_1 = (1,2,3) \qquad b_2 = (1,2,4) \qquad b_3 = (1,2,5) \qquad b_4 = (1,2,6) \qquad b_5 = (1,3,5)
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Using the Ising hamiltonian for each configuration, the magnetic field term gives $-3B$ for every configuration, while the interaction term is positive if the two sites are both occupied or both unoccupied. i.e. we start from $-7J$ and gain $2J$ for every wall:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    Z_1 = e^{3\beta J + 3\beta B} \qquad Z_2 = Z_3 = Z_4 = e^{-\beta J + 3\beta B} \qquad Z_5 = e^{-5\beta J + 3\beta B}
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In other words we have one situations with two walls, three situations with four walls and one situation with six walls. Summing over each of them and multiplying by seven for the translation invariance, we get:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    Z = e^{3\beta J + 3\beta B} + 3e^{-\beta J + 3\beta B} + e^{-5\beta J + 3\beta B} = 
    \\
    = e^{3\beta B} \left(
        e^{3\beta J} + 3^{-\beta J} + e^{-5\beta J}
    \right)
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{The free energy and the partition function}
The Helmholtz free energy is defined using the Legendere transform over the energy
\begin{equation}
    F(T, V, N) = U(S, V, N) - TS
\end{equation}
We can calcualte the expected entropy using its defineition
\begin{equation}
    S = - k_B \ln(\Omega)
\end{equation}
Where $\Omega$ is the multiplicity of the macrostate. We can define all macrostates of the system $i$ where $p_i = \Omega_i / \Omega$ is the probability of the macrostate and get
\begin{equation}
    \langle S \rangle = - k_B \sum_i p_i \ln(p_i)
\end{equation}
Now, using the Boltzman factor as the probability, we get
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \langle S \rangle = - k_B \sum_i p_i \ln\left(\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}\right) = 
    - k_B \sum_i p_i \left[-\beta E_i - \ln Z\right] =
    \\
    = - k_B \left[-\beta \sum_i p_i E_i - \ln Z\sum_i p_i \right] =
    k_B \beta \langle E \rangle + k_B \ln Z
    \\
    T \langle S \rangle = \langle E \rangle + k_B T \ln Z
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Comparing this to equation (11) we get
\begin{equation}
    F = -k_B T \ln Z
\end{equation}

\subsection{1D model with frustration}
Let the hamiltonian of a triangular ladder be
\begin{equation}
    H = -J \sum_i \sigma_i^L \sigma_{i+1}^L + \sigma_i^U \sigma_{i+1}^U + \sigma_i^L \sigma_i^U + \sigma_i^U \sigma_{i+1}^L
\end{equation}
Let $\tilde{\sigma}_i$ be the superspin in $\R^4$ that encodes the information about $\sigma_i^L$ and $\sigma_i^U$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \tilde{\sigma}_i = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \rightarrow \sigma_i^L = 1, \sigma_i^U = 1
    \qquad
    \tilde{\sigma}_i = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \rightarrow \sigma_i^L = -1, \sigma_i^U = 1
    \\
    \tilde{\sigma}_i = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \rightarrow \sigma_i^L = 1, \sigma_i^U = -1
    \qquad
    \tilde{\sigma}_i = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \rightarrow \sigma_i^L = -1, \sigma_i^U = -1
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The then partition function can be written as
\begin{equation}
    Z = \sum_{\tilde{\sigma}_1} \cdots \sum_{\tilde{\sigma}_N} \prod_i \bra{\tilde{\sigma}_i}M\ket{\tilde{\sigma}_{i+1}}
\end{equation}
Where M is the transition matrix
\begin{equation}
    M = \begin{pmatrix}
        e^{-4\beta J} & 1 & e^{-2\beta J} & e^{2\beta J} \\
        1 & 1 & e^{2\beta J} & e^{2\beta J} \\
        e^{2\beta J} & e^{2\beta J} & 1 & 1 \\
        e^{2\beta J} & e^{-2\beta J} & 1 & e^{-4\beta J}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
This makes the particle function
\begin{equation}
    Z = \Tr(M^N) = \sum_i^N \lambda_i^N
\end{equation}
And in the thermodynamic limit, only the highest eigenvalue remains. This changes based on weather $J$ is positive or negative. The eigen values are (from higest to lowest)
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
    \lambda_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}\right)
    \\
    \lambda_2 = \frac{1}{2}\left(-2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1-e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}\right)
    \\
    \lambda_3 = \frac{1}{2}\left(-2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} - e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1-e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}\right)
    \\
    \lambda_4 = \frac{1}{2}\left(2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} - e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}\right)
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
We can see them in figure \ref{fig:correlation-distance}:
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{eigenvalues-4.png}
    \caption{The eigenvalues of the transfer matrix M as a function of $J \beta$. Since $\beta > 0$, the right half of the plane shows the ferromagnetic case while the left half of the plane shows the anti-ferromagneticfor both ferromagnetic ($J=1$) and anti-ferromagnetic ($J=-1$) cases. \newline purple = $\lambda_1$, red = $\lambda_2$, black = $\lambda_3$, gren = $\lambda_4$}
    \label{fig:eigenvalues}
\end{figure}
For the ferromagnetic case, $J=1$, the two middle eigenvalues $\lambda_2, \lambda_3$ are not defined in the region $\beta > 0$, because of the negative value in the square root. But, in both cases $\lambda_max = \lambda_1$:
\begin{equation}
    F/N = \frac{1}{2}\left(2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}\right)
\end{equation}
Now let us find the correlation function $\langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle$. Without loss of generalty, let $i=1$. The correlation function is, by defineition
\begin{equation}
    \langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{\tilde{\sigma}_1} \cdots \sum_{\tilde{\sigma}_N} \bra{\tilde{\sigma}_1}AM\ket{\tilde{\sigma}_{2}}\cdots\bra{\tilde{\sigma}_{r}}AM\ket{\tilde{\sigma}_{r+1}}\cdots\bra{\tilde{\sigma}_N}M\ket{\tilde{\sigma}_{N+1}}
\end{equation}
Where
\begin{equation}
    A = \begin{pmatrix}
        1 & & & \\
        & -1 & & \\
        & & 1 & \\
        & & & -1
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
    \langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle = \frac{1}{Z} \Tr(AM^{r-1}AM^{N-r+1})
\end{equation}
To perform the power over the matrix $M$, we need to diagonlize it $M = C^{-1}DC$:
\begin{equation}
    \langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle = \frac{1}{Z} \Tr(AC^{-1}D^{r-1}CAC^{-1}M^{N-r+1}C) = \frac{1}{Z} \Tr(CAC^{-1}D^{r-1}CAC^{-1}M^{N-r+1}) = \frac{1}{Z} \Tr(BD^{r-1}BD^{N-r+1})
\end{equation}
Where $B = CAC^{-1}$. Writing $M$ using its eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, the matrix multiplicaiton becomes a linear combination of products of two eignvalues in each terms $\lambda_i^{r-1} \lambda_j^{N-r+1}$. Assigning $Z = \lambda_1^N$, every term that doesn't have $\lambda_1^{N-r+1}$ is negligable, and we are left with the linear combinations of the terms that include it:
\begin{equation}
    \langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle = a_1 + a_2 \left(\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right)^{r-1} + a_3 \left(\frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_1}\right)^{r-1} + a_4 \left(\frac{\lambda_4}{\lambda_1}\right)^{r-1}
\end{equation}
Since in our superspin model this is a 1-D Ising model with closest neighboor interaction, there is no phase transition and the correlation should decay as $r\rightarrow\infty$ . Therefore $a_1 = 0$. Moreover, we can neglect the smaller ratios and get the well known relation
\begin{equation}
    \langle\sigma_1^L \sigma^L_{1+r}\rangle = e^{-r/\xi} \qquad \xi = \frac{1}{\ln\left(\frac{\lambda_{max}}{\lambda_{second}}\right)}
\end{equation}
For the ferromagnetic case $J=1$, we remember that $\lambda_2$ is not defined, and therefore $\lambda_{second} = \lambda_4$
\begin{equation}
    \xi = \left[\ln\left(\frac
        {2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}}
        {2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} - e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}}
    \right)\right]^{-1}
\end{equation}
And for the anti-ferromagnetic case $J=-1$:
\begin{equation}
    \xi = \left[\ln\left(\frac
        {2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1+e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}}
        {-2e^{2\beta J} + 1 + e^{-4\beta J} + e^{-4\beta J}\sqrt{\left(1-e^{2\beta J}\right)^3\left(4e^{4\beta J} - 3e^{\beta J} + 1\right)}}
    \right)\right]^{-1}
\end{equation}
Those correlation distances can be seen as a function of $\beta$ in figure \ref{fig:correlation-distance}:
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{correlation-distances-4.png}
    \caption{The correlation distance $\xi$ as a function of $\beta$ for both ferromagnetic ($J=1$) and anti-ferromagnetic ($J=-1$) cases. \newline green = ferromagnetic, blue = anti-ferromagnetic}
    \label{fig:correlation-distance}
\end{figure}
Unsurprisingly, in both cases, the correlation length is zero at the hot limit ($\beta = 0$) and diverges at the cold limit ($\beta = \infty$).
